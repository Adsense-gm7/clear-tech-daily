Split Squat: Balance, Depth, and Leg Position

By Raouf Rouissa · Updated October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

The split squat trains one leg at a time while both feet remain on the floor. It can build leg strength and expose side-to-side differences without requiring the balance of a walking lunge.

Safety note: This article provides general exercise education, not medical advice. Stop if you experience sharp pain, dizziness, chest pressure, or unusual shortness of breath.

Find your stance

Stand with the feet on two separate tracks rather than a tightrope. Step back far enough that both knees can bend without the front heel lifting. Use a rack or wall for balance while learning.

Descend vertically

Lower the back knee toward the floor while the front knee travels in the same direction as the toes. Keep pressure through the whole front foot. A modest forward torso angle is normal and may increase hip involvement.

Choose depth and emphasis

Use the deepest controlled range that is comfortable. A longer stance and more torso lean often increase hip demand; a shorter stance and upright torso generally increase knee demand. These are tendencies, not rigid rules.

Progress the movement

First remove unnecessary hand support, then add repetitions, then hold dumbbells. A rear-foot-elevated split squat is a later option, not a requirement. Keep the pelvis level and avoid bouncing off the bottom.

Your first practice session

Keep the feet on separate tracks and hold a fixed support so balance does not decide the set.

Suggested starting dose: 2 sets of 6–10 per side. Treat this as a starting example for a generally healthy beginner, not an individual prescription. Rest until you can repeat the movement with control, and finish before technique breaks down.

A decision during your set

If balance fails before the legs tire, retain hand support. Removing it is optional, not a prerequisite for adding leg strength.

Record stance and support. Begin with the less coordinated side and match controlled repetitions.

Choose the next step

Supported split squat → more controlled range → dumbbells. Stay at the current step until your repetitions or holds are repeatable. Progression is optional on a tired day; using an easier version can keep practice consistent.

Is hand support cheating?

Hand support can reduce balance demands so you can focus on the legs. Keep the amount of support consistent between sessions. You do not need to remove it if your goal is controlled leg training rather than balance practice.
