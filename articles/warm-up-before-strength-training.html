How to Warm Up Before Strength Training

By Raouf Rouissa · Updated October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

A warm-up should prepare you for the session without becoming a separate workout. Most lifters need a short period of general movement followed by practice sets of the exercises they are about to perform.

Safety note: This article provides general exercise education, not medical advice. Stop if you experience sharp pain, dizziness, chest pressure, or unusual shortness of breath.

Start with general movement

Use three to five minutes of walking, easy cycling, or another comfortable activity. The goal is to feel warmer and more alert, not tired.

Address a specific limitation

If a joint or movement feels stiff, add one or two drills that directly relate to the upcoming exercise. Avoid a long collection of stretches with no clear purpose.

Perform ramp-up sets

For the first compound lift, complete several lighter sets while gradually approaching the working load. As the load rises, lower the repetitions so the warm-up does not create unnecessary fatigue.

Example before squats

After general movement, perform bodyweight squats, then an empty-bar or light goblet set, followed by two or three progressively heavier sets. A beginner using light dumbbells may need fewer steps than an experienced lifter using a heavy barbell.

Avoid these mistakes

  • Turning every warm-up into intense conditioning.
  • Adding prolonged or intense stretching that leaves the upcoming lift feeling weaker or less controlled.
  • Making large jumps to the working weight.
  • Ignoring pain that grows as the warm-up continues.

Separate practice from working sets

For a planned light goblet squat, try a few comfortable bodyweight repetitions, a light set of five, then reassess. Heavier work may need additional gradual steps. Stop each practice set well before fatigue changes the movement.

The final practice set should make the planned load feel familiar. If it produces growing pain or unusual symptoms, warming up longer is not a reason to push through them.

Evidence behind the training framework

Currier and colleagues, 2023: resistance-training prescription systematic review and network meta-analysis. This review compares training approaches in healthy adults. The worked examples above are editorial applications, not experimental results.
