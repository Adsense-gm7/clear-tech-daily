Lat Pulldown: Grip, Setup, and a Controlled Pull

By Raouf Rouissa · Reviewed September 17, 2026 · 9 min read

The lat pulldown develops vertical pulling strength and can prepare you for pull-ups. Grip variations matter less than a stable setup, a comfortable shoulder path, and enough control to keep the cable moving smoothly.

Safety note: This article provides general exercise education, not medical advice. Stop if you experience sharp pain, dizziness, chest pressure, or unusual shortness of breath.

Choose the grip

Start with a shoulder-width or slightly wider overhand grip. A neutral grip is also reasonable when available and may feel more comfortable at the wrist or shoulder. Extremely wide grips shorten the movement and are not required to train the back.

Lock in the setup

Adjust the thigh pad so the legs remain secure without discomfort. Sit tall with the feet flat. Use only a small backward torso angle and keep it consistent; turning the exercise into a swinging row changes the task.

Pull and return

Begin by allowing the arms to lengthen without losing control. Pull the elbows down toward the sides while bringing the handle toward the upper chest. Stop before the shoulders roll forward or the torso leans farther back. Return slowly until the elbows are long.

Mistakes to correct

  • Pulling behind the neck, which is unnecessary for effective back training.
  • Using hip drive to start every repetition.
  • Pulling the bar far below the chest by rotating the shoulders forward.
  • Letting the weight stack crash between repetitions.

Programming and progression

Begin with 2–3 sets of 8–12 controlled repetitions, leaving two or three good repetitions in reserve. Add repetitions before load. When every set reaches the top of the range twice with stable technique, use the smallest practical load increase.

Exercise discomfort is not a requirement. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, chest pressure, or unusual shortness of breath, and seek qualified care for persistent symptoms.
