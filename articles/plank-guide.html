How to Plank: Build Trunk Endurance With Good Position

By Raouf Rouissa · Updated October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

The plank is an isometric trunk exercise: the body resists extension while the forearms and feet support it. Longer is not always better. A shorter, well-braced hold is more useful than a long hold maintained by sagging through the lower back.

Safety note: This article provides general exercise education, not medical advice. Stop if you experience sharp pain, dizziness, chest pressure, or unusual shortness of breath.

Build the position

Place the elbows under the shoulders and forearms on the floor. Step the feet back and form a line from head to heels. Lightly squeeze the glutes and bring the ribs toward the pelvis without rounding aggressively.

Movement reference

Breathe while braced

Create abdominal tension as if preparing for a cough, then continue taking small controlled breaths. Holding the breath for the entire set can cause lightheadedness and is unnecessary for general trunk-endurance training.

Choose the right level

Elevate the forearms on a bench or place the knees on the floor for an easier version. Progress by lowering the support, extending the lever, or adding controlled limb movement—not merely chasing several-minute holds.

Dose the exercise

Use 2–4 sets of 15–45 seconds and stop before position changes substantially. Rest 45–90 seconds. If you can easily exceed the range, choose a harder but controllable variation.

Your first practice session

Use an elevated forearm support or knees-down version if you cannot hold position while breathing.

Suggested starting dose: 2–3 holds of 15–30 seconds. Treat this as a starting example for a generally healthy beginner, not an individual prescription. Rest until you can repeat the movement with control, and finish before technique breaks down.

A decision during your set

If your position changes at 18 seconds, record 18 seconds. Rest, then use an easier version rather than extending a sagging hold.

Record seconds and variation, not repetitions. Add a few controlled seconds before choosing a harder variation.

Choose the next step

Elevated or knees-down → full plank → longer lever. Stay at the current step until your repetitions or holds are repeatable. Progression is optional on a tired day; using an easier version can keep practice consistent.

Does shaking mean the hold is effective?

Shaking can accompany effort, but it is not a score or a target. Continue only while you can breathe and hold a repeatable position. An easier version with control is more useful than extending a failing hold.
