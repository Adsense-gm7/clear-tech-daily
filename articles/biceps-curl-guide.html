Dumbbell Biceps Curl: Elbow Position, Grip, and Control

By Raouf Rouissa · Updated October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

A curl is simple, but small setup choices determine whether the elbow flexors do the work or momentum takes over. A controlled dumbbell curl provides an accessible starting point and lets each wrist rotate naturally.

Safety note: This article provides general exercise education, not medical advice. Stop if you experience sharp pain, dizziness, chest pressure, or unusual shortness of breath.

Start with a comfortable grip

Stand with the dumbbells at the sides and palms neutral or slightly forward. As you curl, rotate toward a palm-up position if comfortable. Keep the wrists aligned rather than bending them backward.

Move at the elbow

Bring the dumbbell upward while the upper arm remains mostly beside the torso. A small amount of shoulder movement is normal, but the elbow should not repeatedly travel far forward to shorten the lift.

Lower completely

Lower until the elbow is nearly straight and the biceps remain controlled. Do not drop through the bottom or force the joint into a painful end range. Use alternating or simultaneous repetitions according to preference.

Choose variations intentionally

Hammer curls use a neutral grip and train the elbow flexors differently. Incline curls place the shoulder behind the torso; preacher curls add external support. No single version is required, and comfort should guide selection.

Your first practice session

Keep the wrist comfortable and start without a backward torso lean. Use a neutral grip if rotating palm-up is uncomfortable.

Suggested starting dose: 2 sets of 8–12. Treat this as a starting example for a generally healthy beginner, not an individual prescription. Rest until you can repeat the movement with control, and finish before technique breaks down.

A decision during your set

If the tenth repetition requires hip drive, count the nine controlled repetitions and keep the same load next time.

Record alternating or simultaneous repetitions and count per arm consistently.

Choose the next step

Comfortable grip → more controlled repetitions → load. Stay at the current step until your repetitions or holds are repeatable. Progression is optional on a tired day; using an easier version can keep practice consistent.

Should both arms always lift together?

Alternating and simultaneous curls can both be used. Alternating can make it easier to focus on one arm, but it also changes the rest each arm gets. Keep your counting method and chosen version consistent.
